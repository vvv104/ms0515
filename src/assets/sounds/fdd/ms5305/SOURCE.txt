Elektronika MS 5305 - the drive of the MS 0515
==============================================

Cut from a recording of the drive itself:

  "Eliektronika MS 5305 - soviet floppy disk drive /
   Radziecka stacja dyskietek / Советский дисковод"
  by CzasowAnton
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ErbxoO4FP8

The recording is CzasowAnton's; credit him wherever these files travel, and
ask before shipping them further.

In it the drive lies open on a table, wired to a PC, and the camera moves
between the drive and the PC's monitor.  Everything below follows from that:
when the drive is out of shot the microphone is mostly hearing the PC, and
even in shot the PC's fans are nearly as loud as the drive.

What is here
------------
motor_start.wav  the spindle coming up to speed
motor_loop.wav   the disc turning
motor_stop.wav   the disc coasting to a stop
step_in.wav      one step of the head; the same sample both ways
step_out.wav
seek_in_N.wav    the head crossing N tracks, for N = 1 2 3 5 8 12 20 30 45 60 79
seek_out_N.wav   the same the other way, built from different takes

Where each came from
--------------------
The camera position was read off the picture, one frame every quarter second:
the monitor is bright and blue, the drive under a desk lamp is dark and warm,
and mean colour alone tells them apart.  697 of the 1107 seconds have the
drive in shot, and only those were used.

The loop and the run-down are one unbroken take, 1076.2 - 1085.9 s: the only
place in the recording where the camera stays on the drive while the spindle
is stopped, starts, runs and coasts to a stop again.  So they follow each
other as the drive made them, and the fans could be measured on both sides of
the sound wanted and subtracted with nothing guessed.

The spin-up is not that take's spin-up.  Its first turns are louder than the
rest and off the beat, so the knock has to be patched out of them one at a
time, and patching that much of a second leaves something that sounds edited
rather than played.  What is here instead is the loop's own rustle read at a
rising speed: the disc's note follows its rotation, so reading the loop slowly
and speeding up is what the drive does - the sound's centre climbs from 2.5 to
3.4 kHz, where the loop sits.  The reading finishes exactly where the loop's
tail begins, so the file runs into the loop the way the loop runs into itself,
and the two switches measure the same to a hundredth of a dB.

The step is from the formatting run at 362 - 393 s, in shot, where the head
moves one track every 405 ms.  Of the forty-odd clean steps there, the sample
is the one closest to the spectrum they all agree on, with what runs under it
subtracted.  An averaged step - the median spectrum carried by a single phase
- was tried and thrown out: it comes out 7 dB peakier than any real step,
because the phases no longer belong to the magnitudes.

The knock
---------
That drive taps once per turn - 202 ms, 297 rpm - and the tap is a fault of
it, not the sound of a working drive, so it is taken out.  Cutting the knocks
and splicing the gaps gave a rhythm of its own, so instead the whole
revolution is evened out: every frame is filed under its phase of the turn,
the level of each phase is measured across turns, and each is brought to what
the turn holds away from the blow.  What repeats with the spindle goes; the
rustle, which does not, stays - and so does how loud the drive is at any
moment, which is what lets the spin-up and the run-down keep their shape.
Two passes.  The blow was 17 dB above the turn's mean and is now 2.6 dB,
against 0.3 dB for white noise put through the same measurement.  On the
run-down the turns stretch out, so there the knock is followed turn by turn -
297, 262, 217, 163, 132, 112, 68 rpm - and the phase follows it down.

At the spin-up the first turns come 180 and 196 ms apart rather than 202, and
the first blow is 4 dB louder than the ones that follow, so a correction
learned over the whole take under-does them.  There are only a handful, so
they are patched one at a time from what lies either side.  Measured as how
much the level still repeats a turn later, the spin-up now scores 0.17, the
run-down 0.12 and the loop itself 0.35.

The same knock is taken out of the step sample, or it would have ended up
inside it.

The head crossing tracks
------------------------
Playing one recorded click per track is what sounds wrong: identical copies
laid over each other at a fixed spacing comb-filter into a buzz no drive
makes.  The recording has the real thing - when the controller drives the head
track after track the clicks run into one another and the drive rasps for as
long as the move lasts.

How long that is, the recording settles.  Measured as stretches of raised
level, bridging dips of up to 30 ms so that one move cannot come out as two,
the longest move in the whole recording runs 399 ms, and the distribution
stops dead there: that is the full stroke of an 80-track drive, about 5 ms a
track.

So every file is one real move, whole, and the number in its name is how many
tracks that move is worth.  Nothing is joined and nothing is stretched.  An
earlier attempt did both - it built long moves by running short ones together,
to a step rate read out of the MS 0515's own ROM - and came out two and a half
times too long.  Joining is wrong for a second reason as well: a stepper
starts and stops, so the beginning and end of a move are the fullest part of
it, and running two moves together buries a pair of those edges in the middle
where no edge belongs.  That is what could be heard as a click.

Up to four tracks the clicks are still countable, so those files are single
real steps 5 ms apart, no two of them the same recording.  Each move keeps
25 ms past the level that found it, for its own ring.  Both directions get different takes: the same
recording used for a move and for the move back would make the head sound as
though it went the same way twice, which is not what a drive does.  For the
full stroke the recording has exactly two clean examples - the head returning
to track 0 after the formatting run, at 387 s, and one at 935 s - and they go
one each way.

The bottom end
--------------
Measured in the part of the turn the knock never touches, the drive adds
about 1 dB over the fans below 1 kHz and 3 to 6 dB from 1.6 kHz up.  So the
bottom of this recording is the room and the PC, and left in it reads as a
barrel rather than a disc; a smooth skirt from 400 to 1000 Hz takes it out.

Nothing else is shaped.  Notching out each individual band the drive does not
own was tried - it leaves a comb, which sounds worse than the barrel did.  A
rustle has to stay broadband, so the fans are subtracted gently and what is
left of them stays as a faint hiss 10 to 13 dB under the drive, rather than
being scrubbed out at the cost of the top end.

Balance
-------
Two numbers here are not taken from the recording, and they are the only two:
how loud the head is, and how loud the spindle is under it.  Both are set
against the machine's own beeper, which renders at 1500 - the loudest thing an
MS 0515 makes, and the one level in this emulator already judged by ear.

The head's click has one amplitude whatever the move: a move of many tracks is
a train of the same click, so it is denser and louder in average but no louder
in peak.  Every sound the head makes is therefore scaled to one click
amplitude, and the group set so its loudest peak reaches 2000 - rms 280 to
410, peaks 1340 to 1980.

The spindle sits at rms 42.  The recording cannot settle this one: there the
drive lies open on a table with the disc uncovered and the microphone right
over it, while in a machine the disc turns inside its jacket, inside the
drive, inside the case, and the head's knocks reach the listener through the
chassis as well as through the air.  So the rustle is louder in the recording
than it can ever be in use, and this is where it was judged to belong.

Note for anyone judging this by ear: before POST, with no diskette in, the ROM
runs its drive test - motor on, then up to ten tries at reading an address
mark, each failure followed by a Restore and a step.  Every Restore is a move
across all the tracks, so what sounds like a continuous rustle there is the
head, not the disc.
